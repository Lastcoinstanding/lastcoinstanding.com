_A NotebookLM research kit for finding non-referral funding (grants and awards) for a Bitcoin
investment-education site. Grants are the **cleanest** monetization path for LCS: a grant funds the
education directly, so — unlike referrals/sponsorship — it doesn't touch the "not a monetization

## 0. How to use this kit in NotebookLM

NotebookLM now does two things: it **gathers** new sources from the web ("Search the web for new
sources" box, with **Fast Research** / **Deep Research** modes), and it **answers questions grounded
only in the sources in the notebook**. So the workflow is:

1. **Create a notebook** — e.g. "LCS — Grants & Awards Research."
2. **Seed it with the verified sources in Part A** — use **Add sources → Website/Link** for each URL.
   These are the specific, high-value programs already confirmed (below), so they're in the notebook
   regardless of what the web search turns up.
3. **Cast a wider net with Part B** — paste each prompt into the **"Search the web for new sources"**
   box. Use **Deep Research** for the broad landscape prompts and **Fast Research** for the narrow,
   named-target ones. Review what it proposes and import the relevant ones.
4. **Interrogate the whole corpus with Part C** — once sources are loaded, paste the query prompts
   into the **Chat**. NotebookLM only knows what's in the sources, so gather generously in steps 2–3
   before relying on the answers.
5. **Resolve the decisions in Part D** — eligibility hinges on them, so answering them makes every
   query in Part C sharper. Bring the answers back here and I'll help you act on the shortlist.

**One framing note before you start (important):** the two most *education-specific* programs each
carry an eligibility gate — see Part A. Your **entity status** (individual vs. for-profit vs.
nonprofit) and whether you'll **open-license** any content are the pivotal variables. The kit is
built to surface exactly those, so keep Part D in mind throughout.

## Part C — In-notebook query prompts (interrogation)

Once sources are loaded, paste these into **Chat**. They're written for a solo-run, US-based Bitcoin
*investment-education* website that publishes free interactive tools/calculators (audience =
prospective investors/allocators), currently a personal/for-profit project, content not open-source.
Adjust the italic descriptors if Part D changes.

1. **Eligibility matrix (the workhorse).**
   `Build a table of every grant, fund, and award in my sources. Columns: Program | Funder | What it
   funds | Eligible applicants (individual / for-profit / nonprofit / open-source-required?) | Typical
   amount | Geography | Deadline or cadence (rolling vs. windows) | Application format | One-line fit
   for a solo-run, US-based Bitcoin investment-education website with free tools/calculators (not
   open-source, audience = prospective investors). Cite the source for each row.`

2. **Bucketed fit assessment.**
   `Sort every program into three buckets: (A) Apply as-is — I plausibly qualify today; (B) Apply if I
   change one thing — e.g., open-license my content, or set up/partner with a nonprofit; (C) Not a fit
   — and say why. For bucket B, name the exact change each program requires.`

3. **What winners have in common.**
   `From any announced grantees or award winners in the sources, extract the common patterns — what
   kinds of projects, people, and framings get funded by each program? What would I need to resemble?`

4. **Independence / strings-attached audit.**
   `For each program, list the obligations and restrictions that come with the money: open-source
   mandates, reporting, exclusivity, ideological or mission alignment, IP terms. Flag anything that
   could compromise editorial independence, neutrality, or my ability to later pursue an
   asset-management partnership.`

5. **Deadline & cadence calendar.**
   `Give me a chronological calendar of application windows and deadlines for every program in the
   sources, noting which are rolling. Flag anything closing in the next 90 days.`

6. **Application-packet requirements.**
   `List the specific questions, documents, and materials each program requires in its application, so
   I can prepare one reusable packet that covers the common ground.`

7. **The core narrative (draft).**
   `Draft the 150-word case I'd make in an application: why a rigorous, no-hype Bitcoin
   investment-education site with free interactive tools deserves grant funding. Then flag where my
   for-profit intent or future monetization plans could conflict with each funder's stated mission,
   and suggest honest framing that doesn't hide the ball.`

8. **The eligibility-unlock analysis (ties to Part D).**
   `Compare the funding I could access in each of these scenarios: (a) as an individual/for-profit as I
   am now; (b) if I open-license my content under a permissive license; (c) if I establish or partner
   with a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. For each scenario, which programs open up, roughly how much total
   funding, and what would it cost me in effort or independence?`

9. **Gaps & what's missing.**
   `Based on the sources, what relevant funders, fellowships, or awards am I probably missing? Suggest
   specific new search terms and categories to run through "Search the web for new sources."`

10. **Recognition & credibility shortlist (not just money).**
    `From the sources, list every recognition/credibility opportunity — awards, best-of lists, research
    prizes, speaking slots, pitch stages, writing contests, fellowships. Rank them by (a) credibility
    value for building thought leadership, (b) whether it's earned/peer-judged vs. pay-to-play, and (c)
    Bitcoin-only vs. crypto-broad mission-fit for a bitcoin-maximalist, high-integrity brand. Flag
    anything tied to BSV or other forks/altcoins to avoid. For the top 3, tell me how and when to
    nominate/apply and what a strong submission would need.`

## Part D — Decisions that gate the research (please resolve)

The best-fit programs each hinge on these, so your answers sharpen everything in Part C. None require a
final commitment now — even a "leaning toward X" helps:

1. **Entity status.** Is LCS a **personal/individual** project, a **for-profit** (LLC/sole prop), or
   would you consider a **nonprofit** (501(c)(3)) vehicle? → Block's Discovery Grant is nonprofit-only;
   several others are individual-friendly.
2. **Open-licensing.** Would you open-license some content/tooling (e.g., Creative Commons content,
   open-source calculators)? → OpenSats' education track effectively requires it. This is the single
   biggest unlock for the most on-mission Bitcoin funder.
3. **Geography / framing.** US-focused is assumed. Some funders (HRF, Block) frame around
   **financial freedom / underserved communities** rather than *investors* — are you comfortable
   framing (parts of) LCS that way, honestly?
4. **Independence red lines.** Any conditions you'd refuse on principle (e.g., a funder's ideological
   alignment, content control, exclusivity) — so we filter those out early?

## Notes & caveats
- Grant programs, amounts, and deadlines **move** — treat everything here as a starting map to verify
  live in NotebookLM, not settled fact.
- Keep the two "gates" front of mind: **OpenSats = open-license**, **Block Discovery = nonprofit-only**.
- Awards ≠ grants: separate cash-bearing prizes from pure recognition (recognition still feeds the
  awareness goal, which is the upstream constraint on all monetization).
- This is the cleanest-integrity path, but stay alert to mission-fit framing: an *investment*-education
  site asking a *financial-freedom/inclusion* funder for money needs an honest bridge, not a costume.
