## Strategic context — JM's revised preference ordering (2026-07-21)
This reframe changes the whole calculus below, so it leads. JM's actual preference ordering:

1. **Grant-funded independence at ~$150k/yr (or more)** → JM would then **relax the AM ambition
   entirely.** Most-preferred outcome if achievable.
2. **The "bridge" strategy** (educational bridges from the explorations to best-of-breed, bitcoin-only
   products; for-profit; **full control retained**) → JM's **preferred fallback** if grants can't
   sustain the site. JM finds it *more useful, innovative, and control-preserving* than an AM role.
3. **Asset-management partnership** → now a **contingency**, the *least*-preferred of the three.

**Consequence:** the original version of this doc treated the AM partnership as the north star and
optimized to preserve optionality toward it. That assumption no longer holds — AM optionality no longer
dominates the decision. The two paths JM actually wants (grant-funded independence; the bridge model)
both keep control, and the open-source lane — previously deprioritized *only* because it threatened the
AM moat — moves to the front.

## Governing principle (revised)
Still non-negotiable: pursue only funding that preserves **independence and editorial neutrality**;
never a hidden monetization funnel; the rigor is the pitch. **What changed:** the constraint is no
longer "protect AM optionality above all." The goal is **durable independent funding at a livable level
(~$150k/yr target)**, pursued through the two control-preserving paths — grants (now including
open-source) and/or the bridge model — while continuing to **exclude the nonprofit route** on integrity
and structural grounds.

## The two viable primary paths

### Path 1 — Grant-funded (open-source is now the anchor, not a "later call")
- **OpenSats (education track) + Spiral** move from *deliberate-later* to **leading candidate for Goal #1**.
  Rationale: they are the most realistic route to *sustained, Bitcoin-native* grant income (they fund
  education contributors at renewable, living-wage-ish levels), and the only reason they were parked —
  protecting the proprietary-tools moat for a paid AM product — has largely dissolved now that AM is
  optional *and* the preferred fallback (bridges) doesn't depend on closed-source tools.
- **Cost that remains (be honest):** open-sourcing erodes some bridge *differentiation* (open calculators
  are cloneable); the OpenSats free/no-funnel ethos sits awkwardly beside a referral model (keep the
  grant-funded education cleanly separate from any bridge monetization); admin/reporting burden;
  renewable, not guaranteed.
- **Also in this path:** Geyser + V4V (clean, do-now — see below) and vetted for-profit/digital-publisher
  grants.

### Path 2 — The bridge model (for-profit, full control) — now elevated to a primary path
- Previously a backlog theme; now JM's **preferred fallback and arguably preferred over AM.** That makes
  the **partnerships / referrals policy** (long-flagged) genuinely important — it's the integrity
  guardrail for the path JM most wants to keep open, not a hypothetical.
- The backlog "bridge" ideas (**Cadena** lending, **River** buying/interest, **Unchained** IRA custody)
  rise in priority accordingly.
- **Honest caveat:** bridges reintroduce exactly the referral/funnel dynamic POSITIONING §3 warns
  against. JM's own mitigation (only leading, highest-integrity, bitcoin-only partners; educational
  bridge not bare referral; disclose honestly) is right — but it must be **written down as policy before
  the first link ships.** So the bridge path is control-preserving but *not* integrity-free; it trades
  the AM-neutrality risk for a referral-neutrality risk that has to be actively managed.

### The one path to keep excluded — Nonprofit
- **Still set aside — and now for stronger reasons.** The AM-conflict reason has softened (AM is
  optional), but two reasons remain and one is new:
  - The "under-resourced communities" framing is a **costume** — an *integrity* objection, independent of
    AM. Unchanged.
  - A 501(c)(3) **cannot run referral/affiliate revenue for private benefit** → converting to nonprofit
    **forecloses the bridge model**, which is now JM's *preferred fallback*. So nonprofit conflicts with
    Path 2, not just the old AM idea.
  - It's also a poor route to *stable personal income* (project-based, competitive, huge admin: board,
    audits, IRS; you'd be a salaried ED, not an owner).

## The key tension to navigate: open-source ⟷ bridge
The two paths pull opposite ways on one question: the **grant path (OpenSats) wants your tools OPEN**;
the **bridge path benefits from DIFFERENTIATED tools** (so bridges are uniquely yours). Reconcile by
**bifurcation:** open-source the *foundational* calculators (unlocks OpenSats) while your
*differentiated, live-updated interactive explorations + brand + traffic* stay the moat that powers
bridges. And note open tools don't actually kill bridges much — bridges monetize **trust, traffic, and
curation, not tool-exclusivity**.

**Low-regret sequencing insight:** open-sourcing foundational tools *opens* the grant door without
*closing* the bridge door (brand/audience remain yours; you can keep new work proprietary). By
contrast, **nonprofit conversion is the one irreversible move that closes the bridge door.** So the
low-regret path is: build reach → open-source foundational tools + Geyser/V4V → keep bridges as an
addable monetization layer → never convert to nonprofit.

## Honest reality check on the ~$150k/yr target
No single lane reliably delivers $150k. Geyser/V4V at that level implies a large, generous, engaged
audience (top-tier-educator territory); OpenSats is the closest thing to a stable *anchor* but is
competitive and renewable; bridge referral income scales with traffic volume. So ~$150k realistically
requires **stacking** (open-source grant anchor + Geyser/V4V + bridge referrals + occasional creator
grants) and, above all, **reach**. Treat $150k as a *stacked* target gated by audience size, not a
figure any one grant hands you.

VC/accelerators (dilutive).

## The throughline: reach is upstream of everything (now even more so)
Every path depends on audience/reach — Geyser needs backers, OpenSats favors demonstrably high-*impact*
(i.e., widely-used) education, Satos is won by mobilizing voters, a speaking slot is earned by a track
record, bridge referral income scales with traffic. So the single highest-leverage move is still **not
applying to anything yet — it's building reach** (the backlog's YouTube/Substack-per-page push + the
Creator Credibility Kit). Under the revised strategy this is *more* central, not less: reach is the
common input to all three futures. **Sequence: build reach → funding + recognition + bridge leverage all
follow.**
